We killed the daily standup eight months ago and nothing bad happened. No missed deadline traced back to it, no surprise at the end of a sprint, no quiet drift. The thing we were most afraid of losing turned out to be the thing we noticed least.

What replaced it was smaller than we expected. People post a line in the channel when they are blocked, and someone picks it up, usually inside the hour. Once a week we spend twenty minutes on what actually changed. That is the whole system, and it has held.

A standup is a status meeting that survived by being renamed. It was invented for a co-located team that could unblock each other in fifteen minutes standing up, and it stopped describing us about three years ago, around the time half the team moved to other time zones. We kept running it anyway, because it had a name that sounded agile rather than administrative.

The cost of keeping it was never the fifteen minutes. It was that six engineers arranged their mornings around a meeting that told them nothing, and the deep work window that a standup sits in the middle of is the only one most of them get.
