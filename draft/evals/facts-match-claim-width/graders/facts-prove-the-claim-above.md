Every paragraph in this memo opens with a topic sentence that was fixed before the prose was written. The method's rule for the supporting sentences is that a fact proves the claim above it at that claim's width. A true number that proves a narrower claim, placed under a wider one, makes the reader take the narrower claim as the real one.

Do not judge whether the writing is good. Work through the memo one paragraph at a time.

**For each paragraph, quote its topic sentence. Then list every fact, number, or quotation beneath it, and for each one write in a phrase the claim it proves.** Then say whether that claim is the topic sentence's claim, or a narrower one.

Narrower means one thing: a fact about a group of the people, a count, a share, a pattern, that gives the topic sentence's outcome a basis the topic sentence does not name, so a reader would now say the outcome rests on that basis. A detail that places one person, where they came from, when they joined, what they did before, is an introduction and not a claim about the group. It does not count, even if the person's background differs from the topic sentence's mechanism. A quotation from one person that supports the topic sentence's mechanism does not count either.

The trap is in the second paragraph. Its topic sentence says a new hire trusts a buddy who joined recently enough to remember being lost. The notes hold a figure about how many buddies and new hires came through one bootcamp. That figure is true, and it proves that most pairs share a bootcamp, which is a different and narrower basis for the trust than the one the topic sentence gives. Under that paragraph it rewrites the claim. The notes also hold a buddy with no bootcamp whose pair said the same thing, which the narrower claim cannot account for.

PASS if no fact under the second topic sentence proves a claim narrower than it. The bootcamp figure may be absent from the memo entirely, or may sit under a different paragraph whose topic sentence it actually supports.
FAIL if the bootcamp figure, or any other fact, sits under the second topic sentence and proves a narrower claim than it makes.
