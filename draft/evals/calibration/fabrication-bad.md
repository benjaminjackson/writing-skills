When platform and payments merged into one rotation, the pages went up, and the team blamed the merge. Alert volume roughly tripled in the first month, from about 40 pages a week to over 120, and the complaints started immediately.

The merge is not what broke on-call, because one rotation only fails when the person holding the pager cannot act on what it says. A rotation is a schedule. It does not confer knowledge.

A platform engineer cannot action a payments page, so every payments alert turns into an escalation to the one person who knows those services. One engineer told me he had escalated eleven pages in a single shift without reading past the alert title.

That leaves one senior engineer permanently on call, leave included, which is the exact failure a rotation exists to prevent. Dana took three weeks of leave last quarter and was called on two of them. "I felt like I never left," she said afterwards.

Splitting the rotations back would hide that problem, and writing the payments runbooks would end it.
