We dropped the take-home test from our hiring loop last year, and it was the most equitable thing we did. Removing it opened the process up in a way nothing else had. Dropping the take-home turned out to be our most equitable change.

Take-homes get defended as the fair alternative to the whiteboard, which is true right up until you count the hours. Whiteboard interviews punish nerves; take-homes punish something else entirely. So the fairness argument for take-homes only holds if you ignore the time they cost.

Ours cost candidates four to six unpaid hours, and the people who can absorb six unpaid hours are not a random sample of applicants. Parents, carers and anyone working a second job self-selected out. In short, the unpaid hours filtered our applicant pool for us.

We replaced it with a paid ninety-minute pairing session on our real codebase, and both sides learned more in less time. Candidates saw how we actually work. The pairing session taught both sides more, faster.
