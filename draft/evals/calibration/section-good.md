# Search Plan

## Why warm introductions do the work

A warm introduction is worth more than a strong application, and the reason is narrower than people usually give: it changes who reads your name first.

An application enters a process, and a process starts with a screener whose job is to make a pile smaller. Very little of what you've done in eleven years survives that, because the reduction runs on titles and years and keywords. The screener isn't making a mistake. They're doing the job they were given, which is to cut.

When someone inside forwards your name, you skip the pile. You show up as a person a colleague vouched for, and the first question in the room becomes "what's she like to work with" instead of "does this match the spec."

The practical version is short. Write the introduction email yourself, in the third person, so whoever forwards it can paste it and add one line. Two paragraphs, no attachments, and one sentence on why this organization and not another.

That leaves the question of who to send it to, and the answer is not who you think.
