# Search Plan

Three of your four conversations came from people you already knew. The recruiter brought one, your own network brought three, and that ratio has held every week since we started. It isn't a small sample and it isn't luck. Your network, in other words, is producing almost all of your conversations.

The recruiter process is the one to stop paying for. You met a screener, then a second screener, and the role description moved twice between the two calls. Nobody in that chain can tell you what the job actually is. So the recruiter process is not worth more of your time.

Twelve of your fourteen organizations are still live. Two have run a hiring cycle for your role and closed it in the last five months, so they come off the list. Ten of the remaining twelve have someone in your network one hop away.

A warm introduction beats a strong application because it changes who reads your name first. An application starts with a screener whose job is to cut a pile on titles and years. Very little of your last eleven years survives that cut.
