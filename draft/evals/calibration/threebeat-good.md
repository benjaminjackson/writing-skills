We dropped the take-home test from our hiring loop last year, and it was the most equitable thing we did.

Take-homes get defended as the fair alternative to the whiteboard, which is true right up until you count the hours. Whiteboard interviews punish nerves, and everyone agrees that is a bad filter. Take-homes punish available time instead, and that filter is harder to see because it operates before anyone applies.

Ours cost candidates four to six unpaid hours, and the people who can absorb six unpaid hours are not a random sample of applicants. Parents, carers, and anyone holding a second job read the brief and quietly withdrew.

We replaced it with a paid ninety-minute pairing session on our real codebase, and both sides learned more in less time. Candidates watched how we argue about a change before they took the job rather than after.
