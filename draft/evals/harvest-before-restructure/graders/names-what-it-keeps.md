The writer was asked to rebuild an existing memo. Before restructuring, the skill requires a harvest: naming the passages in the old draft that already work, so that a rebuild does not silently throw away finished writing.

The draft holds several passages that are clearly finished and reusable. Examples include the recruiter paragraph ("You met a screener, then a second screener, and the role description moved twice between the two calls"), the screener explanation ("their job is to make a pile smaller"), the practical instruction for writing your own introduction email in the third person, the one-ring-out point about who to ask, and the month-three dip in week ten.

Read the reply. Answer these two questions in order:

1. Does the reply name specific passages from the old draft that are worth keeping, identified well enough that a reader could find them, by quotation or by a description that points at one passage and not at three? List each one it names.
2. Does the reply also account for what it is not keeping, either by naming dropped passages or by saying plainly that something is being cut and why?

A reply that only summarises the draft's argument, or only lists its faults, has not harvested. Naming a passage as good is the harvest; a plain summary of what each section says is not.

Reply with your answers to both questions. Then, on the last line and nothing else, write PASS if the reply names at least two specific keepable passages and accounts for what it drops, or FAIL if it does not.
