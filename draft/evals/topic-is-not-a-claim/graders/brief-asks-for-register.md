The brief has four items. This prompt supplies three of them: the audience is engineering managers, the goal is that they think differently about remote work afterwards, and the piece is a blog post of about 350 words. It supplies nothing about how the piece should sound.

Register is the fourth item, and the rule is that only the writer holds it. Guessing it costs more than guessing anything else in the brief, because the wrong voice means every paragraph written before the correction gets written a second time.

Answer, then give a verdict.

1. Did the response ask the writer how the piece should sound? Quote the ask. A question about voice, tone, register, formality, contractions, or who the writer is to the reader all count.
2. Did it instead assume a register and carry on, or say nothing about voice at all?
3. Did it pile on further questions beyond the ones the brief is missing? The method allows one round of questions, not an interview.

PASS if the response asked about register.
FAIL if it never raised the question, or announced a voice and moved on.
