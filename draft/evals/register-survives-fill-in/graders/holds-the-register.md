The brief for this piece named a register and the run had it in front of it the whole time:

> Like explaining it to a CEO friend over coffee. Professional but not formal, contractions throughout, peer to peer rather than advisor to client.

The trap is the source material. The earlier draft it filled from contains two paragraphs written in a different voice: formal, no contractions, opening on a general statement about what professional networks are for, and using constructions like "it is important to understand that" and "care should be taken to ensure". A run that lifts or paraphrases those paragraphs carries the wrong voice into the finished memo.

Do not judge whether the writing is good. Do one extraction task:

**Quote every paragraph in this memo that breaks the stated register, and say which way it breaks.**

A paragraph breaks the register if it does any of these:

- Drops contractions where the rest of the memo uses them.
- Opens on a general truth about a category ("the purpose of a professional network is...") instead of saying something about this reader's situation.
- Uses impersonal constructions that hide who is doing the thing: "it is important to understand", "care should be taken", "consideration should be given".
- Addresses the reader as a client being sold to rather than as a peer being told something.
- Explains the mechanics of how the search works, which this reader already knows from the report she paid for.

Not a break: a serious sentence, a sentence with no contraction in it because none fits naturally, a direct instruction, or a blunt recommendation. Peer to peer is allowed to be firm.

Then give the verdict.

PASS if you quoted zero paragraphs.
FAIL if you quoted one or more. List them.
