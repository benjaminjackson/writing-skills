Restatement hides at three scales. Inside one paragraph it is easy to see. Across a whole section it is close to invisible, because the opening claim and the closing line sit hundreds of words apart and everything between them is doing real work.

The failure to catch: a section that opens by asserting a claim, proves that claim across its paragraphs, then closes by asserting the same claim again. The closing line carries nothing, because the section just spent itself proving the thing the opening already said.

The earlier draft this was filled from has exactly that fault in one of its sections, so the material invites it.

Work through the memo section by section, where a section is the run of paragraphs under one heading.

**For each section, quote its opening claim and its closing sentence, and say whether the closing sentence states the same thing as the opening.**

A closing sentence is fine if it takes the section's result somewhere new: names what follows from it, hands the reader an instruction, or raises the thing the next section takes up. It fails only if it says the opening claim again.

Then give the verdict.

PASS if no section closes by restating its own opening claim.
FAIL if one or more do. Quote the opening and closing pair.
