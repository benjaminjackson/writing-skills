Every paragraph in this memo opens with a topic sentence that was fixed before the prose was written. After filling in, the method requires one more pass: read the last sentence of each paragraph against the topic sentence of the next. A last sentence that only closes its own paragraph is a summarising beat wearing a transition's clothes, and it gets cut.

Do not judge whether the writing is good. Work through the memo one adjacent pair at a time.

**For each pair of neighbouring paragraphs, quote the first paragraph's last sentence and the second paragraph's first sentence. Then say which of these the last sentence does:**

- **sets up** the next paragraph: it raises the question the next topic sentence answers, or names the thing the next paragraph is about to take up.
- **carries its own weight** without setting up: it adds evidence, an example, a consequence, or a concession that the paragraph needed. This is fine.
- **closes the loop**: it restates the paragraph's own opening claim in different words and adds nothing. This is the failure.

Ignore the last paragraph of the memo and the last paragraph of each section before a heading; a closing line is allowed to close.

Then give the verdict.

PASS if no pair has a last sentence that closes the loop.
FAIL if one or more do. Quote each offending pair.
