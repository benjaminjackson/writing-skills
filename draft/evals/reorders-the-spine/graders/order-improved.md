The baseline is the original four-paragraph draft. The produced version is the corrected sentence outline the run wrote in its place, one line per intended paragraph.

The baseline has a real structural fault. It opens on an anecdote presented as a result ("we killed the daily standup and nothing bad happened"), then spends its second paragraph on the replacement, and only reaches the premise in its third: a standup was built for a co-located team and stopped describing this team three years ago. A reader has to carry an unsupported claim through two paragraphs before anything justifies it, and a reader weighing the same decision gets a testimonial rather than a reason.

Judge whether that fault is fixed. There is more than one correct fix and this grader does not care which one was used. Two that both work:

- The premise moves ahead of the claim it supports, so the reason arrives first.
- The anecdote stays first but the outline names it as an anecdote and supplies the reason immediately after, so the reader is never asked to accept it as evidence.

What does not work: the anecdote first, then anything other than the reason, with the premise still arriving later as though the reader had been waiting patiently for it.

Answer, then give a verdict.

1. Where does the reader first meet a reason to believe the opening claim: in the line right after it, or several lines later?
2. Does the outline read as one line of reasoning, each line following from the one before?
3. Was the substance preserved? Material the run has quoted and held aside in a block for the fill-in stage counts as preserved, not dropped; that is the method working. A visible gap marker asking the writer for something is correct behaviour, not a loss. Only a topic sentence stating something the baseline does not support counts as invented.

PASS if the reader gets the reason before being asked to carry the claim, and the substance survived.
FAIL if the premise still arrives late with nothing bridging to it, if the reorder made the reasoning harder to follow, or if a topic sentence was invented.

Judge order only. Do not reward the produced version for being shorter, and do not penalise it for being an outline rather than finished prose.
