The baseline has a real structural fault: its third paragraph carries the premise ("a standup is a status meeting that survived by being renamed", invented for a co-located team, stopped describing this team three years ago) that the first paragraph's claim depends on. The reader meets the conclusion before the reason for it.

Answer, then give a verdict.

1. In the produced outline, does the premise about what a standup is for, and when it stopped fitting this team, now appear BEFORE the claim that killing it cost nothing?
2. Does the produced outline read as one line of reasoning, where each line follows from the one before it?
3. Was the substance preserved? Nothing invented, nothing dropped that carried a distinct idea.

PASS if the premise moved ahead of the claim it supports and the substance survived.
FAIL if the order is unchanged, if the reorder made the reasoning harder to follow, or if new material appeared.
