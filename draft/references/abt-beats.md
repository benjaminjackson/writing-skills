# Beats: one And, But, Therefore per section

Shared method for building, from an outline, a draft whose job is to get the reader to do
something: give, book, sign, reply. `skeleton` runs it at stages 2 and 3. `critique` and
`tighten` use the fact test and the diagnosis list on a section that is not landing.

## What a beat is

A beat is one section, or one main idea, told as three claims in Randy Olson's ABT shape: And,
But, Therefore. The And is the ground the reader already stands on. The But is the belief the
section overturns, or the tension the reader did not know was there. The Therefore is what
follows: what we do, or what the reader does. A case for support, a memo, a talk: each section
gets one beat, and the piece is the beats in order.

Three claims, not three paragraphs. A beat can fill four paragraphs or one. How many paragraphs
is decided at fill-in. How many claims is decided here, and the answer is three.

## Build it from the But outward

Write the But first, then the Therefore, then the And. Never in reading order.

The But is the reason the section exists. If you cannot say what the reader believes now that
this section has to change, there is no section, only a heading with facts under it. So find
that belief first, and write it about the reader, not about us. Then test it: say the belief in
the reader's own words. "I am too small a donor to matter" has tension, and a Therefore can
answer it. "Money is complicated" has none, because nobody holds the opposite.

Three Buts that fail:

- **The But about us.** Capacity, smallness, a limit in our own measurement. Stated as the turn
  of a section it reads as a confession, and a confession is not a turn.
- **The But with no tension.** The reader already agrees, so nothing is overturned.
- **The But that recites the reader's world back to them.** "The rooms you already sit in reach
  immigrants we never will." The reader knows their own rooms. This is the sales-rep trap in
  outline form.

A But often already exists in the outline, mislabelled. A sentence with tension in it sitting
in the And position ("Financial information is free and everywhere, and it has not closed the
gap") is the But, and the section is out of order.

Then the Therefore. It answers the But, and it answers it literally: the two lock on a shared
noun, absence then presence. "Nobody at home who learned it first" is answered by "the person
who learned it first", and any Therefore that does not repeat the But's key noun out loud has
to be checked for whether it answers it at all. Test it for distinctiveness too. If any
organization in the field could say the same sentence ("the internet is untrustworthy, so our
staff teach"), it is not yet the Therefore; find what only we do and say that. It has to come
from the material. If the material does not say what follows, leave the slot open in brackets,
name whose answer it needs, and move on. An invented Therefore looks like a plan and arrives
with no evidence attached, so nobody checks it. One run wrote "the giving ladder runs by time"
into a slot the source files never supported. It read well, which is the problem.

Then the And. It states the section's ground in one claim, never a detail from inside the
section ("Our founder teaches the cohort herself" is a detail; the ground is what the reader
already believes). It has one job: give the But something to take away.
End it on what the person in it wanted, or on what the reader assumes is true. "She took care of
her own money the whole time, and wanted a comfortable life here" hands the But its target. "She
taught herself" closes the paragraph and leaves the But nothing to overturn.

## The claims are topic sentences

Each of the three is a claim, subject and verb, that someone could disagree with. A statistic is
not a claim. "In 2025, 76 immigrants graduated" is a number. "Most of them left with their money
somewhere it grows" is the claim the number supports, and the number goes under it.

Then read every claim in the piece in order, aloud, with nothing else in front of you. That is
the spine. Three things break it:

- **A pronoun with no antecedent in the spine.** "What limits a one-person organization is who
  she knows" reads fine under its paragraph and fails aloud, because "she" was introduced three
  paragraphs of prose ago. Name the actor in the claim.
- **A connective doing the work the order should do.** Every claim opening with "So" or "But"
  means the beats are labelled, not built. Two or three per piece is the ceiling. The other
  joints come from the order, and from an echo word carried from one claim to the next: "that
  conversation", "handed it to her".
- **A claim that is a statistic, a label, or a category.** See above.

## Framing, for a piece written to get the reader to do something

From Anat Shenker-Osorio's work on persuasive framing, the rules that changed sentences in
practice:

- **Name who acts, with an active verb.** "Out of the Mattress handed it to her", never "access
  was provided".
- **Value, then problem, then ask.** The reader agrees with the value before they meet the
  problem.
- **Write how people talk.** If the claim could not be said aloud to a friend, it is not the
  claim.
- **Do not frame in the negative.** "The research we did not run" plants the thing you are
  denying. Say what was done.
- **No villain when the action is a gift.** A donor reads a villain as a reason to sit out.
- **One idea per sentence.** A sentence carrying three ideas is three claims nobody sorted.

## Which facts belong: the reader-action test

A reference doc or an essay is finished when the reader understands. A piece written to get
the reader to do something is finished when the reader does it, so its facts meet a different
bar. Every fact answers one question: how does this make the reader more likely to take the
action the brief names? Write the answer for each fact, in a phrase. A fact with no clear
answer is cut, however true and however hard it was to find.

Run it as an audit before the economy pass, and run it adversarially: one agent per section
lists every fact with its answer and marks the ones it would cut, a second agent tries to defend
each marked fact, and the piece keeps what survives the defense. A single agent asked "is this
extraneous?" keeps everything, because everything is true.

The facts that undermine the case are the ones the audit most often misses, because they are
true and they are ours:

- **A fact that shrinks the problem.** "The rate for foreign-born citizens fell" tells a donor
  the problem is solving itself.
- **A number that makes the ask look wrong.** "$21K carried our first year" three lines before
  a $116K ask.
- **A limit stated as a confession.** "Fifty-five people reported on themselves, with no
  comparison group" is honest and belongs in the piece. Put it where it supports the method that
  replaced it. A bare negative in the middle of the proof reads as an admission.
- **A detail the reader does not need in order to act.** The legal terms, the payment gateway,
  the six months a fiscal sponsorship took. Cut whatever the reader does not need in order to
  decide.
- **A fact that proves a narrower claim than the one it sits under.** A claim about everyone
  we serve, with a number about one group of them beneath it. The number is true and it reads
  as support, but it backs the narrower claim, and the reader takes the narrower claim as the
  real one: the trust, the result, the reach now seem to rest on the trait the number measures.
  It also re-scopes who the piece is for. Check width: every fact under a claim proves that
  claim at that claim's width. One that proves a narrower claim moves to the section that makes
  the narrower claim, and if no section does, it is set aside with the orphan notes.

Each fact lives once, in the section that owns it. When sections are drafted by separate agents,
the same fact and the same introduction of the same person arrive in three places. At assembly,
keep the full introduction where the person first appears (name, age, where they came from,
where they live now, their relationship to us) and re-anchor later mentions with a first name
and one detail: "Tatiana, our graduate in Boston".

## Show, then stop telling

Put the proof next to the claim it proves, and drop the sentence that announces it. "She did
everything right" is a tell. "She worked double shifts, sometimes triple, seven days a week, and
still fell behind people born here" is the same claim shown, and it needs no tell in front of
it. When a transcript or an interview exists, harvest from it before writing around it. The
detail that makes the paragraph is usually already on tape, in the person's own words.

Quotes are verbatim. A paraphrase is marked as one until the speaker confirms it. The speaker is
introduced before they speak, and the attribution comes before the quotation, so the reader
listens instead of working out who is talking.

## When a line is not hitting

Diagnose before rewriting. A rewrite that skips this step reproduces the fault in different
words. The causes, in the order to check them:

1. **The claim is false for its own examples.** "The rooms you already sit in" was meant to
   cover an open house, a giving circle, an employer, and a volunteer, and it was true of two.
2. **A metaphor is stretched over unlike items.** If the image has to mean four things, it means
   none.
3. **The belief overturned has no tension.** Ask what the reader would have to believe for this
   But to surprise them. If the answer is nothing, there is no But.
4. **It recites the reader's world back to them.**
5. **The antecedent is missing.** The line leans on a name or noun that is in the prose and not
   in the spine.

Once the cause is named, rebuild the beat from the But outward. Do not patch the sentence.
