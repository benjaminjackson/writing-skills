# Pitch: Two years of nurse's-office logs from a school that banned phones

The Ridgeway Unified School District, about 9,000 students in six secondary schools, banned phones from first bell to last bell in August 2024. Next month the state senate votes on a bill that would put the same ban in every public school in the state, and its sponsor cites Ridgeway as proof that bans work. That vote is the peg.

I have Ridgeway's own numbers, which nobody has published. Through a public records request I obtained, for each of its six secondary schools and each month from August 2021 to June 2026: nurse's-office visits logged as anxiety, panic or "emotional distress", discipline referrals, and counsellor appointments. Discipline referrals fell by about a third after the ban. Nurse's-office visits for anxiety did not fall; in two schools they went up. The district's press releases mention only the referrals.

I have interviewed 14 teachers, 3 school nurses, 2 principals and 9 students with their parents' consent, and I have the district's internal survey of teachers from spring 2025, also from the records request.

The story is not "phone bans fail". It is what one ban did and did not do, measured, a month before a state copies it on the strength of half the numbers. Readers are parents and legislators deciding the same thing.

Target: a general-interest magazine, about 3,000 words. Draft in three weeks.
