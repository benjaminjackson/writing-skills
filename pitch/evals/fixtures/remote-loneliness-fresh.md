# Pitch: What Harlan County's counselling records say about going back to the office

Harlan County, a 4,000-employee local government, sent almost all of its office staff home in March 2020 and kept them there. In September 2026 the county board voted to order everyone back five days a week from 5 January 2027. The board's stated reason was "isolation and declining wellbeing among remote staff". The employees' union filed a grievance on 2 November and the hearing is set for 14 December. That hearing is the peg.

I have the evidence the board did not use. Through a public records request I obtained monthly counts of sessions with the county's employee assistance programme (the free counselling line staff can call) from January 2018 to September 2026, split by department and by whether the department went remote. Counselling use in the remote departments went up in 2020 and 2021, as it did everywhere, but from 2023 it fell below the 2018-2019 level. In the departments that stayed on site it did not fall. The county's own exit-interview summaries, from the same request, show "isolation" cited by 6 of 212 leavers in 2025.

I have also interviewed 31 county employees, 19 remote and 12 on site, and two of the five board members, one on the record. Several remote workers told me the commute, not their colleagues, was what they had lost sleep over.

The story is not "remote work is fine". It is that a government made a loneliness argument for a policy and its own records do not support it. Readers are managers and workers whose employer is making the same argument right now.
